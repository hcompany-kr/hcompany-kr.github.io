Most guides to buying a recorder are ranked lists. This one is not, because the ranking is not the hard part.

The hard part happens the evening after. You have the recording. Somewhere in it is the two minutes you actually needed.

<p class="pull">Every recorder is a different answer to one question: what makes it start? Everything else follows from that.</p>

## The five kinds, side by side

| | What starts it | What you have after a day | Can you find the moment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wearable, continuous | You, once in the morning | One unbroken file | Only by listening through it |
| Wearable, VOX | Any sound | Many clips, most irrelevant | Yes, but you sort a pile |
| Dedicated recorder | A button, at the time | One file per session | Usually |
| AI note taker | A button, at the time | File plus transcript and summary | Yes, by reading |
| Phone, keyword start | A word you chose | A file that begins at the moment | Yes |

The column that decides most purchases is the first one. The column people regret is the third.

## Wearable recorders: badge, pen, keychain

These exist because of one constraint. In the moment that matters you cannot be seen reaching for a phone.

They solve it by removing the moment entirely. You switch the device on before anything happens — usually at the start of the day — and it runs.

That works, and it is worth understanding what it costs.

**In continuous mode** you get one enormous recording. Makers commonly quote something in the range of 20 to 100 hours of continuous recording per charge, with storage measured in hundreds of hours. At the end of the day the file contains your commute, your lunch, and somewhere in the middle, the exchange you were worried about. There is no index. Finding it means listening.

**In VOX mode** the device waits for sound, records while sound continues, then stops. This is why standby figures get quoted in months rather than hours: it is idle most of the time. The files come out separated and time stamped, which is genuinely easier to work with.

But VOX has a specific weakness that matters here. **It triggers on sound, not on meaning.** A door, a printer and a kettle all start it. A day in an ordinary office produces a great many clips of nothing, and the one you want is somewhere among them.

And because it starts once sound is already present, **the beginning of a sentence is commonly clipped.** The first word of "you're being unreasonable" is the part you lose.

## Dedicated recorders

A purpose built recorder with a real microphone will beat a phone in a large room, at distance, or with several people talking.

This is not a small advantage and it is worth being straight about it. If you are recording a lecture hall, a conference room, or anything where the source is more than a couple of metres away, dedicated hardware wins on audio quality and it is not close.

The trade is that it starts with a button, at the time, which puts you back in the problem the wearables were trying to escape.

## AI note takers

The newer category. A recorder paired with transcription and summarising.

**Plaud** states a credit card sized device that attaches magnetically to the back of a phone, transcription in 112 languages with speaker separation, and 300 free transcription minutes a month with paid tiers above that, at a stated 159 dollars. **TicNote** states 25 hours of continuous recording, three microphones and 600 free transcription minutes a month.

These figures are what the makers publish rather than anything we have measured, and pricing in this category moves.

What they genuinely fix is the search problem. A transcript is readable, and reading eight hours is not the same task as listening to eight hours.

What to check before buying is where the recurring cost sits. **The hardware is a one time purchase; the transcription minutes are not.** A monthly allowance in the low hundreds of minutes covers a few meetings, not a working day, so the useful question is how many minutes a month you would actually generate.

## The phone you already have

Two versions of this.

**The default recorder** on your phone starts with a button, same as everything else, and produces a file from that moment. Nothing to buy, nothing to carry, nothing to charge.

**A keyword triggered app** starts when it hears a word you set in advance. The file begins at the moment, not at nine in the morning, so there is nothing to search through afterwards.

This is the category we work in, so read the next part with that in mind. [TalkSafe](https://hcompany-kr.github.io/talksafe/) is an Android app that starts on a word you choose, works with the screen locked, and saves the 30 seconds before the start along with the file — which is the part that answers the clipped first syllable problem. Cutting a section keeps the original file intact rather than replacing it.

## Where hardware genuinely wins

Being honest about this is the only way the rest of the page is worth reading.

**Audio quality at distance.** A phone in a pocket, under fabric, is a compromised microphone. A dedicated recorder placed on the table is not.

**Long unattended sessions.** If something needs to run for two days, a device with a month of standby does that and a phone does not.

**Situations where the phone itself is the problem.** Somewhere you have handed your phone over, or cannot have it out, hardware is the only option.

**Battery independence.** A recorder that dies does not also cost you your phone.

## Three questions that decide it

**When will you know you need it?** If you will know in advance — a scheduled meeting, a lecture — a button is fine and you should buy on audio quality. If you will only know once someone has already started talking, the start mechanism matters more than the microphone.

**What will you do with it the next day?** If the answer involves finding a specific moment, favour anything that produces either short files or a transcript. A single day long recording is the hardest possible version of that job.

**How often?** Occasional use rarely justifies a second device to buy, carry and charge. Daily use in a room with poor acoustics might.

## In short

The ranked lists sell devices. The thing that decides whether a recording is any use to you is narrower than that: **what made it start, and what that leaves you holding afterwards.**

A recorder that captured everything and a recorder that captured the right two minutes are not the same product, even when the specs look identical.
