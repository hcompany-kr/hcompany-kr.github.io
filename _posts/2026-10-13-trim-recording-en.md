The recording is an hour and forty minutes long. What you need is two minutes somewhere past the one-hour mark.

Nobody is going to listen to the rest. Not a manager, not an investigator, not a judge. If you hand over the whole file, someone has to find the two minutes. If you hand over the two minutes alone, someone will ask what came before them.

<p class="pull">Trimming is easy. What matters is whether the full file is still there after you have done it.</p>

## How the common apps handle it

| App | Where | How you trim | What happens to the original |
|---|---|---|---|
| Google Recorder | Pixel phones | Menu → Crop & Remove, on the audio or the transcript | The edit ends with Save copy |
| Samsung Voice Recorder | Galaxy phones | Drag the trim handles | You choose: replace the file, or create a new one |
| Audacity | Computer | Select, delete, export | The source file is untouched unless you export over it |
| TalkSafe | Android | Select the section and trim | Always kept; the section is saved as a new file |

The difference that matters is in the last column.

## Google Recorder on Pixel

Google's Recorder app trims through **Crop & Remove** in the recording's menu. **Crop** keeps the part you select; **Remove** cuts the part you select. You can make the selection on the waveform or on the transcript, which is the fastest way to find a sentence in a long file. Either way, the step ends with **Save copy**, and you name the new file.

If you have a Pixel, this is a good editor. It is not on other phones.

## Samsung Voice Recorder

On Galaxy phones, the built-in Voice Recorder lets you drag trim handles to the length you want and listen to the section before saving. Samsung's own guide describes the choice you get at the end: **replace the file, or create a new audio file.**

That prompt is the one place to slow down. Replace overwrites the recording you started with. If there is any chance the recording matters later, choose the new file.

## Audacity on a computer

Audacity is free and precise to the millisecond: select the section, delete what you do not need, export. Your source file is not changed unless you export over it under the same name, so export the excerpt under a new name.

The cost is the trip — moving the file off the phone, editing, moving it back if you need it there.

## TalkSafe

[TalkSafe](/talksafe/) trims on the phone and **never offers to overwrite**. The section you select is saved as a new file and the original stays exactly where it was. There is no prompt to get wrong.

Three things follow from that.

- **The beginning is yours to choose.** TalkSafe starts on a word you set in advance and saves the **30 seconds before** that word was heard, so the file starts a little before the moment. Trimming lets you decide exactly where the excerpt begins, while the full lead-in stays in the original.
- **The clip can be cleaned on its own.** TalkSafe's **AI noise removal** works on a trimmed clip. Cut the two minutes first, then clean only those — processed on the device and saved as yet another new file.
- **The clip can go anywhere.** Sending uses the **Android share sheet**, so the excerpt goes to whatever app accepts files: your email, a cloud drive, your computer.

## Why the original is the part you keep

An excerpt is a claim: *this is what was said, and nothing important around it changes the meaning.* The original is how you back that claim up.

**The other side can ask for the rest.** In US federal courts, Federal Rule of Evidence 106 lets an adverse party require that any other part of a statement be introduced at the same time when fairness requires it. Since the amendment that took effect on **1 December 2023**, it covers oral statements as well as written and recorded ones, and the completing part can come in over a hearsay objection. If the rest of the file no longer exists, that is a question you cannot answer.

**The court asks whether the file is what you say it is.** Rule 1003 treats a duplicate as admissible to the same extent as the original unless a genuine question is raised about authenticity. In India, an electronic record needs a certificate under section 63(4) of the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam 2023, and the certificate records the file's hash value. In South Korea, the Supreme Court requires proof that a copy was made without alteration. Every one of these is easiest when the untouched original is sitting on the device that made it.

How courts in fourteen countries treat recordings is in [You have the recording. You still may not be able to use it](/blog/en/recording-evidence/).

## A short routine

1. **Do not touch the original.** No renaming, no re-saving, no converting.
2. **Trim the section that matters**, with a little of what comes before and after.
3. **Clean the clip if you need to.** Noise removal on the excerpt, not the original.
4. **Write a note.** Date, place, who was there, and the time in the original where the excerpt starts.
5. **Back the original up off the phone.** Send it to yourself.

Then submit the clip and the note, and keep the original ready.

## Sending is not the same as storing

The share button does two different jobs. Sending a file to your own email or drive is storage. Sending it to a colleague, a family group or a forum is distributing it, and in many places that is a separate legal question from whether you were allowed to record. [What to do with a recording, and what not to do with it](/blog/en/after-recording/) goes through it country by country.
