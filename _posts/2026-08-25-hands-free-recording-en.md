There is a specific moment this question comes from. A conversation turns, and you realise you should be recording — but reaching for your phone at that exact moment is either impossible or obviously rude.

So: what actually starts a recording without your hands?

<p class="pull">Four methods exist on Android. Only one of them works with the phone in your pocket and the screen off.</p>

## The four options

| Method | Starts on | Works with screen locked | Keeps audio from before |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Widget or shortcut | A tap | No — needs the screen | No |
| Scheduled recording | A set time | Yes | No |
| Voice assistant command | Your voice, spoken to it | Yes, but it responds out loud | No |
| Keyword-triggered recorder | Any spoken keyword | Yes, silently | Yes, with a rolling buffer |

The differences matter more than the table suggests, so here is what each one really means.

## Widgets and shortcuts

A home-screen widget removes two steps — you no longer open the app and press record inside it. But you still have to wake the screen and tap.

Useful when you have a second and a free hand. Useless in the situation that produced the question.

## Scheduled recording

Some recorders let you set a start time in advance. If you know a meeting begins at 14:00, this works, and it is fully hands-free.

The limitation is obvious: you have to know when. Most of the moments people want recorded are not scheduled.

## Voice assistants

Siri, Google Assistant and Bixby can start a recording on command, and they work with the screen locked.

Three problems, though.

**They answer out loud.** The conversation stops and the other person knows exactly what just happened.

**They are tied to your voice.** Assistants are built to recognise their owner. If you cannot speak — which is common in exactly these situations — there is no trigger.

**They start from zero.** Whatever was said before the command is gone.

## Keyword-triggered recording

This is the category built for the problem. The app listens for words you chose in advance and starts recording when it hears one.

[TalkSafe](/talksafe/en/) is an Android app that works this way. Word recognition runs on the device, recording begins **with the screen locked and the phone in a pocket**, and there is no sound or visible change at the moment it starts.

Two properties make this different from the other three.

**The keyword is not tied to your voice.** Detection is of the word itself, regardless of who said it. So you can set what the *other* person is likely to say — a landlord's *deposit*, a dealer's *warranty*, a manager's *contract*. The moment the subject comes up is the moment recording starts, and you did nothing.

**The 30 seconds before are kept.** TalkSafe saves the half-minute preceding the trigger. This solves the problem that ruins every button-based recorder: you usually realise a sentence mattered a beat *after* it was said. With a rolling buffer, that sentence is at the front of the file instead of just outside it.

## What to set as a keyword

There is no universal list, and anyone offering one is guessing. Two rules hold everywhere.

**Few, not many.** Twenty common words will start recordings over lunch. Two or three that belong to a specific situation will not.

**Specific, not generic.** *Problem* appears in any conversation. *Deposit* does not.

A practical trick: set two words likely to appear in the same sentence, so either one triggers it.

## What this app does not do

A notification stays visible for the entire recording and **cannot be switched off**. There is no hidden mode and no disguised icon.

TalkSafe assumes you are recording a conversation you take part in. Whether that is lawful depends on where you are — in the US it varies by state, and there is [a state-by-state breakdown](/blog/en/state-recording-consent/) as well as [an article on how consent works](/blog/en/recording-with-consent/) in the states that require it.

## The short version

If you need a recording to start while your phone is in your pocket and you cannot speak or reach for it, a keyword-triggered recorder is the only one of the four that does it. If you also need what was said just before, the rolling buffer is the part to look for.
