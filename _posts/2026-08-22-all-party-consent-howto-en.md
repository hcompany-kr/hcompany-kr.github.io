Most articles about all-party consent states read like a closed door. Your state requires everyone to agree, therefore you cannot record.

That is not what the statute says. California's law does not prohibit a participant from recording a conversation — it prohibits recording **without first informing** everyone that it is happening.

<p class="pull">All-party consent is a sequence, not a prohibition.</p>

## The strongest form of consent is the simplest one

Ask.

The most legally secure method is to put the question directly: *do you mind if I record this?* If everyone says yes, that is express consent, and there is very little left to argue about afterwards.

There is a weaker version that also works. Courts have found implied consent where a person was told a recording was being made and carried on with the conversation regardless — that is why the announcement at the start of a support call functions the way it does. California's Supreme Court has also left open the possibility that a visible "recording" indicator could serve as adequate notice, with the decision to keep talking counting as consent.

Implied consent is real, but it is the fallback. If you are in a position to ask out loud, ask out loud.

## Where the sequence usually goes wrong

Here is the practical problem with an ordinary recorder.

To capture the agreement, you have to press record **before** you ask. So the other person watches you start a recording and then hears you ask permission — which is an awkward way to open, and it puts the request in the worst possible framing.

If instead you ask first and press record after, you have consent but no record of it. Months later, if they say they never agreed, you are relying on your own account of a conversation that happened off tape.

Neither version puts the agreement inside the file, which is exactly where it is most useful.

## What a keyword trigger changes

[TalkSafe](/talksafe/en/) starts recording when it hears a word you chose in advance. So the request can be the trigger.

Set **record** as a keyword. Then say it:

> "Do you mind if I **record** this?"
> "Sure, go ahead."

The word starts the recording as you say it. The answer arrives a second later and lands in the file. Nothing was pressed, nothing was announced twice, and the consent is now the first thing on the recording rather than a claim you have to support.

That ordering is not available on a recorder with a button, because the button always has to come first.

## The 30 seconds before

TalkSafe also saves the half-minute preceding the trigger, which means a short stretch of conversation from before you asked is attached to the front of the file.

Often that is the point — it is the part that made you decide to record. If you would rather it wasn't in there, **the trim function removes it** and the rest of the file stays intact.

Worth knowing either way, so it is a decision rather than a surprise.

## Notice that cannot be switched off

One more piece fits this situation specifically.

While TalkSafe is recording, a notification stays visible and **it cannot be turned off.** There is no hidden mode, no disguised icon, no way for a user to make the recording invisible.

In a state where the offence is defined by secrecy — recording *secretly or surreptitiously*, in the statute's own words — an indicator the user cannot disable is worth having. It is also the reason the app is built the way it is: the design assumes you are recording a conversation you are in, with the other person able to know.

## The one thing that stays off-limits

Consent does not stretch to cover conversations you are not part of.

Leaving a phone recording in a room and walking out, capturing what colleagues say to each other, setting a device down at a table you then leave — that is interception, it is a serious criminal offence in every state, and permission from one person does not cure it. This is the line that carries prison time, and it is the same line in all fifty states.

Everything else on this page is about the other side of it.

## In practice

Ask before you start, or make the asking start it. Keep the answer in the file. Trim the front if you want to. Don't record rooms you are not in.

Which states require all of this, and which only require your own consent, is set out in [the state-by-state breakdown](/blog/en/state-recording-consent/). If the recording is happening at work, [there is a separate question](/blog/en/workplace-recording-policy/) about what your employer's policy can and cannot do — the two are decided independently.
